# bonding_overview

I did extensive measurements on two bonded devices that enables a better, higher detail view into the mechanisms of bonding. This was a (1) 2 devices (one each side) from James first wafer, all shorted with silver paint, connected to MEA23 (old champion) and (2) a real device from the wafer Baran reetched bonded to MEA1k24 (recently regoldpalted by Baran). Especially for (1), i could measure the impedance and connectivity over different pressure levels. For (2), I did not go very far, because I saw shorts early on. Later found these were not realted to pressure, but there from the beginning (device). So creation of shorts is still unsure, when and where they can happen really. 

My take is the following. 2 major sources of variability are PI device quality, especailly etching the flaps free, and no shorts. And 2nd the gold pillar quality + cleanness during assembly. Without systematic proflier data, it's hard to disambiguate the two.

Bonding happens in the follwing way with our spring pads: metal1 pads (fabricated first, far away from CMOS after flipping) show a high likelyhodd of forming an intitial connection with the gold surface. ALthough they are 1um further away from the CMOS than metal2 pads. THeir advantage is that their flaps are only 1um thick, improving elasticitiy, and likely, the etching works better here, because the "pit" that needs to be etched isn't as deep. Although they show a connection early, it's not a low impedance one. Only very rarely have i seen connection with now pressure. With light pressure connectivity improves here. One common pattern we see is that it can go to voltage-connectivity of 0.8 or 0.95, very bimodal. This number realted the input impedance of amplifier, 112MOhm. A value of 0.8 suggest 10's of MOhm connection. 0.95 is potentially a low impedance bond (a few Ohms), or around 1-5 MOhm. metal2 pads fall into either of these 2. Interstingly pads that go to the 0.9 range, stay there even if pressure increases. On the other hand, if pads first show up in the 0.95 connecvitiy range, but with high impedance (1-5 Mohm), pressure can bring these pads to a proper bond. This can be seen as a pressure limit: If one see's high connectivity (0.95) but high high impedance (1-5MOhm) pressure can fix this for both metals. Increasing the pressure engages more pads only in the beginning, and already connected pads increase in number of electrodes (becomes bigger) with more and more pressure. But critically, many pads go to 0.8 and not 0.95 (half roughly). This varies alot per area on the chip. For example on MEA1K23, bottom left corner looked very good, other side didn't. We don't know if this is due to gold surface or pad quality, but i am leaning towards gold quality.

metal2 pads behave differently. ALthough reetched on MEA24 bond, and correct from the beginning for the MEA23 bond, metal1 pads don't connect early. They require higher pressure to connect. But benifit more from pressure. Increases here lead to more electrodes with connectivitiy >0.8 than for metal1. They show the same pattern that a high impedance (1-5MOhm) bond can be improved with pressure. I have some more interesting data for metal1. On MEA24, there was a big difference in bonding quality (0.8 vs 0.95) bewteen the two devices/ the two halfs of the chip. Wheras metal1 pads went mostly to 0.95 for both halfs, metal2 pads went to 0.8 for the bottom half of the device, and to 0.95 as well for top half. So what is different about the bottom part here? Luckily, have images of it, just need to look at. 

All of this is fun and nice, but what do we really need to use thiese findings for? For the new design. We won't have springs anymore, enough of them, but metal2 badly etched pads kind of resemble that design, and it only worked well once (MEA23 4+4 device which still had the etching issue.)
